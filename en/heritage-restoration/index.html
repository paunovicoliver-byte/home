Heritage & Restoration

Preserving what deserves to remain.

Old wooden windows should not automatically be replaced. Many of them were made with a knowledge of wood that is worth keeping — together with the material itself.

Talk to us about a historic building

  2. Heritage & Restoration

Understand first. Then decide.

Over the years we have worked on the restoration of old houses and on historic wooden windows for public buildings, libraries and schools, as well as on traditional architecture.

Every one of these projects began the same way: by looking closely at what was already there.

Before any decision

What we look at.

  1. 01

    Original construction

    How the element was built and how its parts are joined.

  2. 02

    Profiles

    The mouldings and bars that give a façade its character.

  3. 03

    Proportions

    How the window or door relates to the wall and to the other openings.

  4. 04

    Material

    Which wood was used and how it has aged.

  5. 05

    Condition

    Where the element has suffered and how deep the damage goes.

  6. 06

    Architectural character

    What role the element plays in the building as a whole.

Then

Repair, reproduce or replace.

  • Repair

    Where the wood is sound, we repair — keeping the original material and the work of those who made it.

  • Reproduce

    Where parts or whole elements are beyond saving, we reproduce them faithfully, with the same profiles and proportions.

  • Replace

    Only where it is truly necessary — and then in the same architectural language, so the building keeps its face.

European heritage projects

Our experience includes work connected with projects supported through the European Heritage Hub Small Grants Scheme — a scheme associated with Europa Nostra, the European Union and the ALIPH Foundation.

DRVENO is an independent family workshop. This reference describes project experience; it does not imply endorsement by, or an institutional partnership with, any of these organisations.
