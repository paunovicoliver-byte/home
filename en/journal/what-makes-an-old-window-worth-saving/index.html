What makes an old window worth saving?

Peeling paint and a draught do not mean a window has reached its end. Before deciding anything, we look closely.

20 May 2026 · 1 min read · DRVENO workshop

Peeling paint, a draught, a sash that sticks in winter. To many people, these are signs that a window must go. Often they are only signs that it needs attention.

We look before we decide

Before we recommend anything, we try to understand the window in front of us:

  • Construction — how it was built and how its parts are joined.
  • Profiles — the shapes of its mouldings and bars, which give a façade much of its character.
  • Proportions — how the window relates to the wall and to the other openings.
  • Material — what wood was used, and how it has aged.
  • Condition — where it has suffered, and how deep the damage goes.
  • Architectural character — what role the window plays in the building as a whole.

Repair, reproduce or replace

Only then do we decide. Where the wood is sound, repair keeps the original material and the work of the people who made it. Where parts are beyond saving, we reproduce them. And where a window truly has to be replaced, we try to do it in the same language, so the building keeps its face.

Preserving what deserves to remain

Not everything old is precious. But a great deal of it is better than it looks at first sight — and much of it deserves to remain.
